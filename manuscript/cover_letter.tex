\noindent Dear Editor-in-Chief,

\vspace{0.5em}
I am submitting the manuscript \textbf{``Counterfactual Attribution of Crypto
Systemic Stress on an Auditable On-Chain Graph''} for consideration in
\emph{Digital Finance}. Crypto-asset markets have repeatedly transmitted distress
between exchanges, lending protocols, and stablecoin issuers, most recently in
the liquidation cascade of October 2025. Measures of this contagion commonly
infer the network from price co-movement, using CoVaR-type or marginal expected
shortfall statistics. The resulting graphs are estimated rather than observed,
and the attributions built on them cannot be checked against the transfers that
moved funds between entities. Public blockchains record those transfers, but the
raw ledger becomes an entity-level graph only after addresses are resolved to
institutions.

The manuscript treats the entity-resolved on-chain ledger as an auditable
candidate skeleton for a linear structural causal model of entity stress. On this
model it defines On-Chain Causal CoVaR and a counterfactual loss attribution. The
analytic results are central. The interventional effect of one entity's stress on
another entity's tail measure is a network multiplier that depends on the lagged
coefficients and the directed paths, not on the flows or the shock distribution.
This yields flow-free bounds and implies zero attribution between entities with
no directed path. The attribution is invariant to how event-window shocks are
abducted, so that choice affects only the hinge loss. The linear Shapley
attribution has a closed-form walk decomposition, and an exact subset algorithm
computes it with $2^p$ rather than $p!\cdot p$ model evaluations. The paper gives
equal weight to the auditable ledger-based attribution and to its application to
the October 2025 cascade, with checks on the FTX (2022) and USDC/SVB (2023)
episodes. In that application the ranking differs from $\Delta$CoVaR (Spearman
0.52) and remains stable under coefficient perturbation (mean Spearman 0.88 across
25 perturbations). The analytic checks refer to the daily specification. The
advantage of the observed graph over a price-estimated graph at hourly frequency
is small and not consistent across specifications, and I report it as weak
evidence.

The paper fits the scope of \emph{Digital Finance} on three counts. It addresses
systemic risk in crypto-asset markets and how that risk is measured. It addresses
supervisory transparency, since each attribution traces back to public ledger
records and an explicit model. It also provides reproducible inputs: the code, the
query definitions, and the derived result files are openly available. Raw query
outputs are regenerated from public Dune Analytics data.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration at any other journal. It is single-authored, and the author declares
no competing interests. The code and derived results are publicly available at
\url{https://github.com/vinhqdang/VEIN-On-Chain-Causal-Risk-Attribution-for-Cryptocurrency-Systemic-Risk}.
Thank you for considering this manuscript.
